# GATE 2027: what the organisers have published

Read from the official site on the `checked` date above. The official site
is the authority: check it again before relying on a date, and correct this
year's facts for one learner by writing the same keys to
`learner/gate/2027.md` (it overrides this file key by key).

## Dates

- Exam days: 6, 7, 13, 14, 20 and 21 February 2027 (Saturdays and Sundays).
- Results: 19 March 2027.
- Which day and session a given paper falls on is published by the
  organisers with the paper-wise schedule and on the admit card. Until the
  learner records it (`profile set gate.exam_date.CS 2027-02-..`), the
  engine counts down to the first exam day, which is the safe assumption.

## Pattern

- 65 questions, 100 marks, 3 hours, computer based.
- General Aptitude: 10 questions, 15 marks, in every paper.
- CS (Computer Science and Information Technology): General Aptitude 15,
  Engineering Mathematics 13, subject questions 72.
- DA (Data Science and Artificial Intelligence): General Aptitude 15,
  subject questions 85.
- Question types: multiple choice (MCQ), multiple select (MSQ) and numerical
  answer (NAT), each worth 1 or 2 marks.
- Negative marking applies to MCQ only: a wrong 1-mark MCQ costs 1/3 mark, a
  wrong 2-mark MCQ costs 2/3 mark. MSQ and NAT carry no negative marks, and
  an MSQ earns nothing unless every correct option and no wrong option is
  chosen.

The engine applies exactly these marking rules to GATE items and timed tests.

## What is not published, and how the framework handles it

- **Marks per subject.** Only the General Aptitude total and, for CS, the
  Engineering Mathematics total are fixed. Every other subject weight in the
  tree is a planning prior, shown with a star in `gate status` and
  `gate triage`. Replace priors with counts from official past papers
  (`item import --official`, then `gate weights --apply`) or with a figure
  the learner trusts (`gate weight <subject> <marks> --source "..."`).
- **Marks needed for a rank.** The marks that correspond to a rank change
  every year with the paper's difficulty, the number of candidates and, for
  papers held in more than one session, normalisation. No table here would
  be honest. The target is set per learner at onboarding: the GATE strategist
  looks up the marks-versus-rank data of recent years for the learner's
  paper, records the target with its source (`profile set gate.target_marks.CS 72`),
  adds a safety margin, and says plainly that it is an estimate.

A rank is an outcome the framework cannot promise. What it can do is show,
at any time, the marks that verified practice and mock tests currently
support against the target.
